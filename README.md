**Thanksnare** names a thank-you that arrives before you have accepted a request and makes refusal feel rude. It combines *thank* and *snare*: gratitude becomes a social trap before consent. Say it as “THANK-snare”; both roots keep their ordinary pronunciation.

> thanksnare: A thank-you given before you accept a request, making refusal feel rude.

Run: `python3 main.py`

Requires Python 3.13 and only its standard library. The program writes UTF-8 to `/output/entry.txt`, or to the path supplied through `ENTRY_OUTPUT`, creating parent directories as needed. The output contains only the entry, with no trailing newline.

**Selection: thanksnare replaces yeswince.** Both earn equal novelty estimates, name an immediately recognizable social experience, and define a precise moment in one reading. Thanksnare specifies both the premature thank-you and its effect, so it does not sweep in every use of “thanks in advance.” Its familiar roots form a compact, spellable compound with an ordinary English pronunciation, matching yeswince on formation. The deciding advantage is the pairing: thanks conventionally expresses gratitude, while a snare captures and constrains. That reversal makes a polite gesture's coercive effect tangible. Yeswince's agreement-and-recoil pairing is apt, but less surprising. This is a win at criterion 5 after four ties, not a trade of clarity or word formation for cleverness.
